# Visual restraint

Review the **rendered artifact**, not just its HTML or prompt. Read the
separate checklist of twenty common stylistic tics and additional reasoning
and copy traps at `references/checklist.md`.

A gradient, serif, square card, pill, italic, upper-case label or punctuation
mark is not evidence of AI authorship. The stronger indicators are repeated
recipes with no communication job, decorative complexity and copy that could
describe any product. Respect genuine brand treatments and useful density.

## Audit sequence

1. Identify the artifact's audience, purpose, real content and prescribed
   visual language. Preserve user-approved choices.
2. Inspect at realistic size, then compare screens or slides as a sequence.
   Spot repetitive compositions, weak hierarchy, irrelevant photographs,
   redundant layers, charts whose axes hide information and forced variety.
3. Read titles and subtitles on their own. Flag abstract buzzwords, duplicated
   phrasing, formulaic contrasts, unsupported specificity and manufactured
   authority. Do not invent facts to replace a weak claim.
4. Group issues by impact: misleading evidence or unreadability first,
   then repeated layout patterns, then superficial decoration. Explain the
   mechanism, not a claim that a person or model authored the result.
5. Fix actual source files using the project's design tokens. Reduce elements
   that lack a real job; do not replace every page with an equally generic
   minimalist pattern.
6. Render again, confirm the content and interactions remain correct, and
   give a before/after comparison with real evidence. State what was not
   rendered or tested.

## Before / after illustration

Before: "01 / INSIGHTS" eyebrow, "Unlocking seamless transformation",
three bordered feature cards and a gradient behind a redundant subtitle.

After: a defensible, source-backed observation in the title and a single
visual comparison that explains its mechanism, units or caveat. Retain
whatever brand or interface features demonstrably help the user.

For copy-only requests, prefer an existing copy audit skill; do not invoke
this visual workflow unnecessarily.
